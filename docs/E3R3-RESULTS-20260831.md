## The three findings, each now replicated

1. **Horizon works.** Drift compounds: +1.4% at 5 days, +2.4% at 10 (economic bars
   cleared on raw mean). Leaders genuinely beat random symbols at 10 days.
2. **Stops destroy this signal class at every horizon** (A1<A2, A3<A4, uniformly,
   for REAL and nulls alike): on drifting names, a stop converts temporary dips
   into realized losses. Consistent with E1's loose-stop OOS finding, now general.
3. **The entry day never mattered.** For the third consecutive round, same-symbols-
   at-random-days beats every entry rule tried (A4: −1.62%, CI fully negative).
   Across E2 + three E3 rounds: TEN entry constructions tested, and the information
   was always in the SYMBOLS, never in the DAY.

## What the program has actually converged on

Three pre-registered rounds have, in effect, rediscovered the one robust anomaly
that lives in daily large-cap data — **slow cross-sectional momentum** — and
correctly rejected every attempt to trigger-trade around it. That is not a failed
search; it is the honest answer. Its strategic content:

- **QTP-as-trigger-machine (any flavor) is unsupported** by every test run —
  including its own six months of live history.
- **QTP-as-systematic-portfolio-engine is the supported direction**: hold the
  current top-K momentum leaders continuously, rebalance on a slow clock, no
  stops, no triggers — harvest the drift instead of taxing it with entries.
  Testing that honestly needs a ROUND-4 PORTFOLIO HARNESS (equity-curve backtest
  of a daily-ranked top-K basket vs random-K baskets, turnover and fee
  accounting, weekly rebalance variants) — a different simulator than the
  per-signal engine, buildable on the same bars store, pre-registered like
  everything else. It also dissolves the N2-hindsight critique, because a
  continuous portfolio has no entry day to contaminate.
