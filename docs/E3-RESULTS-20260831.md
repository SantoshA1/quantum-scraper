## What the wreckage teaches (the first data-generated lead)

G1's decomposition is the finding of the round. Its **symbols were good**: the same
names on random days made **+0.342 mean R** (they were the window's strong drifters).
Its **timing destroyed them**: entering at 20-day-high moments subtracted ~0.40R
from its own symbols' base rate, with the full CI negative. Combined with E2 (QTP's
momentum-flavored scanner also anti-selected), two independent momentum expressions
now show the same signature in this tape: **strength-at-highs is an entry tax, not
an edge, in this regime — but strength itself identifies good inventory.**

Round-2 candidate (NOT tested this round, per the no-same-round-modification rule):
*own the strong symbols, enter on pullback days* — e.g., 20d momentum leaders bought
on down-closes rather than at highs. **Honesty constraint: round 2 must NOT run on
these same 69 days** — this hypothesis was generated by them. It requires either a
Polygon daily-bar backfill (~12 months, ~550 symbols, via the documented E1-runner
exception workflow) giving a pre-April out-of-hypothesis segment, or forward
accumulation. Round 2 gets its own pre-registration either way.

## Honest framing of a zero-graduate round

This is the harness working, not the pivot failing. Three strategies that would
each have cost weeks of paper losses to falsify live were disqualified in about an
hour at zero risk, and the round produced a testable, non-obvious hypothesis. The
cost of a wrong strategy is now an afternoon, not a month of red weeks.
