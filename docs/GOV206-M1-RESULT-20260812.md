## 1. The defect, finally located — from the backfill's own trail, not from guesswork

Gov 206 proved the *output* was wrong and named the writer. It deliberately did not guess the
arithmetic. The 08-05 backfill's own correction trail (`quantum.r_multiple_corrections`) records
the denominator it used, which settles it:

**`RECERT_OWN_STOP` divided `net_pnl` by `|entry_fill − stop_used| × qty` where `stop_used` was
the FINAL, TSM-trailed stop at *exit* time — not the entry-time stop.**

| trade | stop the backfill used | actual entry-time stop | denominator used | honest denominator | R written | R true |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ADI | 379.16 *(the trailed stop; exit was 379.2532)* | 391.32 | $94.64 | $463.12 | −1.0276 | −0.2100 |
| **WMB** | **71.68 — five cents from the 71.63 entry** | 73.86 | **$7.40** | $338.92 | **−1.2000** | **−0.0262** |
| WY | 24.26 | 25.05 | $24.11 | $294.80 | −1.6316 | −0.1334 |

The consequence is structural, not random: measuring risk against the stop that *fired* forces
every stop exit to ≈ −1R × local overshoot, by construction. It erases precisely the thing the
TSM exists to do — cut losses to a fraction of entry risk — and it left `r_multiple` and
`risk_amount` in the same row measured against two different stops. (`RECERT_SIBLING_STOP` rows
such as ARE diverged too, via a wrong quantity basis.)

## 2. What shipped

One migration, one transaction, three guards:

1. **Abort if any violating row belongs to another writer** — a divergence not from
   `RECERT_20260805_fills` would be a *new* defect and must not be papered over. (There were none;
   the live writer `H4_EXIT_RESOLUTION_v2` was clean before and after.)
2. **Abort if the violating set exceeds the measured bound** (31 rows).
3. **Abort unless trail rows = updated rows** — the append-only trail and the update cover the
   identical set or nothing happens.

`r_multiple := round(net_pnl / risk_amount, 4)` on the 31 rows. `lineage_source` untouched —
the R3 filters and the short-release condition key on the `RECERT_` prefix. Idempotent: a second
apply finds an empty violating set.
